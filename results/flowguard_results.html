Our lab used bare-SYN attacks (nmap -sS, hping3) — no payload, no completed connection. The packet-size features the model relies on are zero, so every attack flow reads as an empty, benign-looking connection. CIC-IDS2017's attacks were HTTP-based, which populate these features.

Conclusion

FlowGuard genuinely learns the attack types in its training data — 96% recall on unseen flows at a 0.03% false-positive rate, not memorisation. But it does not generalise to attack variants it never saw: a SYN flood is a denial-of-service attack, yet the model misses it entirely because it looks nothing like the HTTP floods it trained on. A dataset-trained NIDS learns the specific behaviours in its data and should not be assumed to detect novel variants of the same attack class.
